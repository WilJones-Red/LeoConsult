**Internal Document**  
**Leo Consult**

## Purpose

The delivery system defines how Leo Consult turns messy data and unclear questions into actionable decisions for clients.

This is not a step-by-step procedure manual. It is a set of decision rules and guardrails that work when it is not just the founder doing the work.

---

## Core Delivery Principle

We deliver decision-focused analytics, not dashboard theater.

Every dashboard, report, and analysis we build must serve a real decision the client needs to make. Beautiful visualizations that do not drive action are not our product. Clear insights that lead to better choices are.

This applies whether we are building basic BI dashboards for a Starter client or custom ML models for an Enterprise client. The deliverable changes, the principle does not.

---

## Standard Engagement Flow

**Stage 1: Align on the decision**

Work with the client to define:

- What decision needs to be made
- Who will make it and when
- What would change based on the answer

If the decision is unclear, do not proceed to analysis. Pause and clarify.

**Stage 2: Assess the data**

Review available data for:

- Accessibility (can we get it?)
- Quality (can we trust it?)
- Completeness (is it enough to inform the decision?)

If data quality fails, pause and address it. Do not analyze bad data.

**Stage 3: Analyze and interpret**

Focus the analysis on the specific decision. Avoid:

- Interesting tangents that do not serve the decision
- Excessive data exploration without a question
- Analysis paralysis

Keep the work tight and decision-focused.

**Stage 4: Deliver findings and recommendations**

Provide:

- Clear summary of what we found
- Honest assessment of confidence level
- Specific recommendation or decision framework
- Limitations and uncertainties

Written summary, not slides. Plain language, not jargon.

**Stage 5: Clarify next steps**

End every engagement with a clear statement of what happens next:

- Client proceeds with decision
- Client needs follow-up work
- Client decides to stop here

No ambiguity.

---

## Decision Rules for Pausing or Stopping Work

**Pause when:**

- Data quality is worse than expected and needs remediation
- The decision changed mid-engagement and we need to realign
- Client access or responsiveness has dropped significantly
- We are stuck and need to reframe the approach

**Stop when:**

- Data cannot be trusted and cannot be fixed in reasonable time
- The client cannot or will not clarify the decision
- The engagement is misaligned with our capabilities
- The client is not acting in good faith
- Continuing would produce misleading or harmful recommendations

Pausing is normal. Stopping is sometimes necessary. Neither is failure.

## Quality Guardrails

**Before delivering any analysis, check:**

- Does this analysis directly inform the stated decision?
- Do we trust the data underlying this analysis?
- Are we clear about confidence levels and limitations?
- Can we explain this to the client in plain language?
- Would we bet our own money on this recommendation?

If any answer is no, do not deliver. Fix it first.

---

## Communication During Delivery

**Regular check-ins**

Update the client on:

- What we are learning
- Any data issues we found
- Whether timeline or scope needs adjustment

Frequency depends on engagement length. Default: weekly for multi-week projects.

**Surface problems early**

If we discover an issue that affects the work, tell the client immediately. Do not wait for the next scheduled update.

**No surprises at delivery**

The final deliverable should confirm what the client already expects based on ongoing communication. If the conclusion is a surprise, we failed to communicate during the work.

---

## Handling Common Situations

**Client asks for additional analysis mid-engagement**

Evaluate:

- Does this serve the original decision?
- Is it within scope and timeline?
- Will it improve the outcome?

If yes to all, consider adding it. If no to any, document the request and propose it as follow-up work.

**Data is worse than initially represented**

Pause. Document the issues. Present options to the client:

- Fix the data and continue
- Adjust scope to work with available data
- Stop the engagement

Let the client decide with full information.

**Client wants a dashboard or ongoing reporting**

Clarify the decision it serves. If there is no decision, decline or reframe. If there is a decision, explain how ongoing reporting supports it and price accordingly.

**Analysis reveals uncomfortable truths**

Deliver it clearly and respectfully. Our job is truth, not comfort. If we would not share difficult findings, we should not take the work.

**Client is not responsive**

After two attempts to reconnect with no response, pause the work and send a clear message:

"We are pausing work until we can reconnect. Let us know when you are ready to continue."

Do not keep working without client engagement.

## Delivery Roles (Early Stage)

**For founder-only stage:**

Founder handles all delivery work. Document decisions and lessons learned.

**For founder + 1 helper:**

- Founder owns client relationship and final quality check
- Helper conducts analysis under founder guidance
- Both align on findings before delivery
- Founder delivers final recommendations to client

**For founder + small team:**

- Founder assigns work and stays involved in key decisions
- Team members own specific analyses
- Regular internal reviews before client delivery
- Founder maintains final quality gate

Do not scale delivery faster than quality control can handle.

---

## What We Do Not Do

**We do not:**

- Deliver analysis without a clear decision attached
- Build on data we do not trust
- Hide uncertainty or limitations
- Create complexity to justify higher fees
- Hand off client relationships to junior team too early
- Automate delivery in ways that compromise judgment
- Deliver what the client wants if it is not what they need

---

## Measuring Delivery Quality

**Good indicators:**

- Client can clearly explain the decision they need to make
- Client trusts our findings enough to act on them
- We identified and addressed data quality issues early
- No major surprises at final delivery
- Client would work with us again

**Warning signs:**

- Client is confused about what we delivered
- We felt uncomfortable with data quality but delivered anyway
- Scope expanded significantly without formal adjustment
- Client ghosted after delivery
- We would not do the engagement the same way again

Learn from both.

---

## System Evolution

As the firm grows:

- Document common delivery patterns
- Build reusable decision frameworks (not templates)
- Train new team members on quality guardrails
- Refine decision rules based on real experience

The system should get clearer, not more complex.

This system exists to protect quality and client outcomes as we scale. Use it.
