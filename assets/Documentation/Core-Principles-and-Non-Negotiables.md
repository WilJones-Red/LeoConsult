# Core Principles & Non-Negotiables

**Internal Document**  
**Leo Consult**

---

## Purpose

This document defines what Leo Consult will not compromise on as it grows. These are the standards that guide every decision, every system, and every hire.

Everything else can evolve. These cannot.

---

## What We Will Not Compromise

**Decision clarity over output volume**

We exist to help clients make better decisions, not to produce reports. If the work does not lead to a clearer decision, we do not do it.

**Trust before speed**

Building trust with clients matters more than delivering fast. We do not cut corners to meet arbitrary deadlines.

**No selling insights we do not believe in**

If we would not bet our own money on a recommendation, we do not give it to a client. No hedged advice, no consultant-speak cover.

**Data quality is non-negotiable**

We do not build analysis on data we do not trust. If the foundation is broken, we say so and stop.

**Honest communication, always**

We tell clients what they need to hear, not what they want to hear. We acknowledge uncertainty. We admit mistakes.

**Small and excellent beats large and mediocre**

We would rather stay small and do exceptional work than grow fast and deliver average results.

---

## What Good Looks Like at Leo Consult

**A good client engagement:**

- Starts with a clear decision to make
- Uses data the client trusts
- Ends with the client more confident about their choice
- Builds a relationship we want to continue

**A good internal process:**

- Has clear decision rules, not rigid steps
- Works when it is not just the founder running it
- Can be explained in plain language
- Protects quality without creating bureaucracy

**A good team member:**

- Asks clarifying questions instead of making assumptions
- Says "I do not know" when uncertain
- Prioritizes client outcomes over personal efficiency
- Can explain complex ideas simply

**A good system:**

- Solves a real problem we have today
- Works for the next stage of growth, not some future state
- Can be taught to someone new in an hour
- Has clear guardrails and decision points

---

## What We Refuse to Scale Poorly

**Selling work we cannot deliver**

As we grow, the temptation will be to take on more clients or larger projects. We only do this if we can maintain quality. Revenue does not justify bad work.

**Complexity for its own sake**

More process, more tools, more hierarchy. None of this makes us better unless it solves a specific problem. We stay as simple as possible for as long as possible.

**Compromising on data quality**

No matter how many clients we have, we do not build on bad data. This standard does not bend.

**Losing founder judgment in critical decisions**

Early on, founders stay involved in client qualification, major project decisions, and quality checks. We do not delegate these too early.

**Hiring to fill roles instead of solve problems**

We add people when we have a specific problem that needs solving, not because we think we should have a certain org chart.

**Dashboard theater**

No matter how much clients ask for it, we do not build dashboards that do not serve a decision. This is core to who we are.

---

## How These Principles Show Up in Practice

**In sales and intake:**

We decline work that does not meet our standards, even if we need the revenue.

**In delivery:**

We pause or stop projects when data quality fails, even if the client wants us to continue.

**In team building:**

We hire slowly and only when the need is clear and urgent.

**In communication:**

We use plain language, acknowledge uncertainty, and do not hide behind jargon.

**In pricing:**

We charge for value, not hours. We walk away from clients who want to negotiate quality down to hit a price point.

---

## When to Revisit These Principles

These principles are designed to last, but not to be rigid forever. Consider revisiting if:

- The market fundamentally changes in a way that makes these principles unworkable
- We discover a principle is creating harm rather than protection
- We have been operating for multiple years and have clear evidence a principle should evolve

Revisiting does not mean abandoning. It means pressure-testing with real experience.

For now, these are the spine.

---

## Using This Document

**For founders:**

When making a difficult decision, ask: "Does this violate a non-negotiable?" If yes, do not do it.

**For new team members:**

This is how we think. If these principles do not resonate with you, this is probably not the right place.

**For clients:**

We do not share this document directly, but clients should feel these principles in how we work.

**For system design:**

Every system we build should reinforce these principles, not work around them.
