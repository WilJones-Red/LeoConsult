**Internal Document**  
**Leo Consult**

---

## Purpose

The sales and intake system exists to:

- Identify clients we can genuinely help
- Protect the firm from misaligned work
- Ensure honest, clear expectations from the first conversation
- Build trust before any engagement begins

This system is not about maximizing volume. It is about matching our capabilities to real client needs.

---

## Core Principles

**Founder-led sales early on**

In the early stages, founders handle all sales conversations. This ensures:

- Direct understanding of client needs and market fit
- Consistent messaging and quality control
- Learning what works and what does not
- Building relationships that matter

**Honesty over optimization**

If we are not the right fit, we say so. If the work is unclear, we acknowledge it. If the timeline is unrealistic, we push back. Honest communication builds better long-term relationships than winning every deal.

**Qualify hard, deliver well**

Better to decline marginal work than to take on a project that will not succeed. Our reputation depends on doing excellent work for the right clients.

---

## Qualification Criteria

A good fit client typically has:

- A specific decision they need to make
- Access to relevant data or willingness to provide it
- Realistic expectations about timelines and outcomes
- Budget aligned with the scope of work
- Authority to act on the recommendations

If any of these are missing, proceed with caution or decline.

## Intake Question Framework

Use these questions in early conversations:

**The Decision**

- What decision are you trying to make?
- Who needs to make this decision, and when?
- What happens if the decision is delayed or made incorrectly?

**The Data**

- What data do you have available?
- Where does it live, and how accessible is it?
- Do you trust the data you currently have?

**The Context**

- What have you already tried?
- What is blocking you from making this decision today?
- Are there political or organizational constraints we should know about?

**The Expectations**

- What does success look like for this work?
- What would make this engagement a waste of time for you?
- What timeline are you working with?

These questions help surface misalignment early.

## Red Flags

Decline or seriously reconsider work if you see:

- Vague or shifting objectives
- No clear decision maker or authority
- Unrealistic timelines relative to scope
- Data that does not exist or cannot be accessed
- Requests for a dashboard without a decision attached
- Client expects us to tell them what decision to make
- Budget misaligned with the actual work required
- Past consultant relationships that ended poorly for unclear reasons
- Unwillingness to provide context or access to key stakeholders

If multiple red flags are present, decline the work.

---

## Decision Rules

After the intake conversation, choose one of three paths:

**Proceed**

Move forward if:

- The decision is clear
- The client is qualified
- We have the capability to help
- No major red flags are present

**Reframe**

Propose a different scope if:

- The ask is unclear but the need is real
- A smaller starter engagement would clarify the path
- The timeline or budget needs adjustment
- The client is open to redefining the work

**Decline**

Say no if:

- We are not the right fit
- Multiple red flags are present
- The client is not ready for this work
- The engagement would harm our reputation or capacity

Declining respectfully is a sign of good judgment, not failure.

---

## Communicating Decisions

**When proceeding:**

Confirm the decision, timeline, and deliverables clearly. Put it in writing before work begins.

**When reframing:**

Explain why the original scope needs adjustment. Propose a clear alternative. Give the client space to decide.

**When declining:**

Be direct and respectful. Offer a brief explanation if appropriate. Suggest alternatives if possible. Do not apologize for having standards.

## Early-Stage Notes

In the first year:

- Founders do all sales and intake work
- Document lessons learned from each conversation
- Refine qualification criteria based on real experience
- Build repeatable language for common situations
- Track which types of clients succeed and which do not

This system will evolve, but the principles stay constant.
