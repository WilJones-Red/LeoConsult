# People & Roles

**Internal Document**  
**Leo Consult**

---

## Purpose

This document defines when and how to add people to Leo Consult, and how to structure roles without creating unnecessary complexity.

We add people to solve specific problems, not to fill an org chart.

---

## Core Principle

Small and excellent beats large and mediocre.

We grow the team only when:

- We have a clear problem that needs solving
- We have tried solving it without adding people
- The need is consistent, not temporary
- We can maintain quality with the addition

---

## When to Add Someone

Add a person when:

**The constraint is clear and urgent**

You can articulate exactly what problem they will solve and how it will free up existing capacity for higher-value work.

**You have work waiting, not hypothetical future work**

There is enough qualified client work to justify the role, not just hope that more will come.

**Quality is slipping due to capacity**

You are declining good work or delivering at lower quality because of time constraints.

**The founder is stuck on low-value work**

The founder is spending significant time on tasks that someone else could do well, preventing work only the founder can do.

**You have clarified the role**

You know what success looks like, what decisions they will own, and how they will work with the rest of the team.

---

## When Not to Add Someone

Do not hire if:

**The problem is temporary**

A surge in work that will normalize in a few months. Use contractors or decline excess work instead.

**The problem is unclear**

You feel overwhelmed but cannot articulate what the person would do. Clarify the problem first.

**You are hiring to look legitimate**

Building a team for appearance rather than necessity. Stay small longer.

**You have not tested the work**

You have not done the role yourself or tested it with a contractor to know what it requires.

**Money is tight**

If losing a client would create financial stress, you are not ready to hire.

## Early-Stage Roles (In Order of Likely Need)

**First hire: Analytical support**

Handles data work, dashboard building, quality checks, and analysis under founder supervision. Frees founder for client relationships and strategic work.

When: Multiple active Starter/Growth subscriptions, founder bottlenecked on monthly reporting and dashboard maintenance.

**Second hire: Client success or account manager**

Handles client communication, monthly check-ins, support questions, and renewal conversations. Shares relationship load with founder.

When: 10+ active subscription clients and founder cannot manage all relationships, onboarding, and new sales simultaneously.

**Third hire: Operations or delivery coordinator**

Manages client onboarding, implementation timelines, documentation, and ensures monthly deliverables go out on time.

When: Administrative overhead and delivery coordination is consuming significant founder time.

After this, growth should be deliberate and problem-specific. No default next hire.

**Capacity planning by tier:**

- Starter clients: 1 analyst can support 8-12 clients with strong processes
- Growth clients: 1 analyst can support 4-6 clients given higher complexity
- Enterprise clients: Typically require 1-2 dedicated team members

Use these as guidelines, not rigid rules. Quality matters more than efficiency.

## Role Structure (Early Stage)

**Founder responsibilities (do not delegate too early):**

- Final client decisions and difficult conversations
- Quality review on all deliverables
- Sales qualification and major deal decisions
- Strategic direction
- Team hiring and performance decisions

**Early team member responsibilities:**

- Defined analytical or operational work
- Client communication under founder oversight
- Process documentation
- Specific project ownership

Clear ownership, but founder stays close to the work.

---

## Decision Rules for Delegation

**Delegate when:**

- The task is repeatable and well-defined
- The person has demonstrated competence in the area
- Mistakes are recoverable and not catastrophic
- You have capacity to review and provide feedback

**Do not delegate when:**

- The task requires deep client trust or difficult judgment
- Quality cannot be easily verified
- The risk of error is too high
- You have not done it enough yourself to teach it

Delegation is not abdication. Stay involved.

## Hiring Standards

**Look for:**

- Clear thinking and communication
- Comfort with ambiguity and unstructured problems
- Intellectual honesty (says "I do not know" when appropriate)
- Low ego, high curiosity
- Alignment with core principles

**Avoid:**

- People who need extensive structure or hand-holding
- Those who optimize for looking good over being useful
- Anyone who talks in buzzwords or jargon
- People uncomfortable with direct feedback
- Misalignment on what matters (speed over trust, volume over quality)

Hire slowly. Fire quickly if misaligned.

---

## Onboarding Expectations

**For the first 30 days:**

- Shadow the founder on client work
- Read all core documentation
- Complete a small real project under supervision
- Demonstrate understanding of core principles

**For the first 90 days:**

- Own a piece of real client work
- Provide feedback on process and documentation
- Show independent judgment on routine decisions
- Build trust with the team

No formal training program. Learning by doing on real work.

---

## Team Communication

**Default to written, async communication**

Use written updates, documented decisions, and clear email threads. Minimize meetings.

**Meetings only when necessary**

- Complex decisions requiring discussion
- Client strategy alignment
- Sensitive feedback or performance conversations

If it can be an email, make it an email.

**Transparency on work and decisions**

Share context broadly. Explain why decisions were made. No secret strategic discussions unless truly sensitive.

**Direct feedback, given quickly**

If something is wrong, say so. Do not wait for a review cycle. Be specific and constructive.

---

## Performance Expectations

**Evaluate team members on:**

- Quality of work (accuracy, clarity, usefulness)
- Judgment (good decisions when operating independently)
- Communication (clear, honest, timely)
- Alignment (consistently upholds core principles)
- Growth (improving over time, learning from mistakes)

**Not on:**

- Hours worked
- Number of tasks completed
- Responsiveness outside reasonable hours

Focus on outcomes and judgment, not activity.

---

## When Someone is Not Working Out

**Act quickly if:**

- Repeated quality issues despite feedback
- Misalignment with core principles
- Inability to operate independently in their role
- Breakdown in trust or communication

**Steps:**

- Give clear, specific feedback early
- Provide one focused chance to improve
- If no improvement, part ways respectfully

Prolonging a bad fit helps no one.

---

## Compensation Philosophy

**Pay fairly and simply**

- Competitive salary for the role and market
- No complex bonus structures early on
- Adjust as the business grows and roles evolve

**Be transparent about constraints**

If cash is tight, say so. If someone is underpaid relative to market, acknowledge it and address it when possible.

**Reward outcomes, not tenure**

Pay increases based on expanded capability and value, not time in role.

---

## Scaling Team Structure

**At 2-3 people:**

Flat structure. Everyone reports to founder. No layers.

**At 4-6 people:**

Consider light role specialization (analysis, client work, operations), but stay flat. No middle management.

**At 7+ people:**

Only then consider adding a layer. And only if absolutely necessary.

Stay as flat as possible for as long as possible.

---

## What We Do Not Do

**We do not:**

- Hire to hit growth targets or look bigger
- Create roles without clear problems to solve
- Build org charts for hypothetical future states
- Add management layers prematurely
- Hire people who do not align with core principles
- Keep poor fits longer than necessary

---

## Common Situations

**Founder feels overwhelmed but cannot articulate the need**

Do not hire yet. Spend a week documenting time and identifying specific bottlenecks. Then decide.

**Great candidate appears but no immediate need**

Pass. Hire for problems, not people. Great candidates will appear again when you actually need them.

**Current team member wants a promotion or title change**

Ask: What problem does this solve? If none, do not do it. If they have genuinely expanded capability, acknowledge it with pay or responsibility, not just title.

**Client wants to know team size**

Be honest. "We are small and intentional about staying that way. You will work directly with experienced people, not junior staff."

---

## Evolution

As the firm grows:

- Document role definitions based on real work
- Build simple onboarding materials from actual experience
- Refine hiring criteria based on who succeeds
- Create clear career paths only when team size justifies it

Do not over-engineer people systems early. Build what you need when you need it.

---

Add people to solve problems, not to fill an org chart. Keep the team small and excellent as long as possible.
