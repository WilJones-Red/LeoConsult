# Internal vs External Systems

**Internal Document**  
**Leo Consult**

---

## Purpose

Leo Consult operates with two distinct layers of information and systems:

- **Internal systems**: How we run the business, make decisions, and improve operations
- **External systems**: What we share with clients to build trust and collaboration

This separation matters because it protects our operational integrity while maintaining transparency where it counts.

---

## What Stays Internal

**Financial details**

Pricing models, profit margins, cost structures, and internal financial planning remain private.

**Sales and qualification criteria**

The specific red flags, decision rules, and scoring we use to qualify clients stay internal. Clients know we have standards, but not the exact framework.

**Capacity and resource planning**

How we allocate time, who is working on what, and our internal workload management is not client-facing.

**Operational processes**

The details of how we run intake, onboarding, project management, and internal reviews are for our team only.

**Strategic planning**

Long-term business goals, competitive positioning, and growth plans remain internal.

**Learning and mistakes**

Post-mortems, lessons learned, and internal critiques are protected. We improve privately.

**Team performance and compensation**

Individual performance reviews, compensation decisions, and internal feedback stay confidential.

---

## What is Shared Externally

**Decision frameworks**

Clients see how we approach their specific problem. We share our thinking process and decision criteria for their project.

**Work progress and findings**

Clients get regular updates on what we are learning, what we found, and where we are in the process.

**Data assessment results**

If we find data quality issues, gaps, or limitations, we document and share them clearly.

**Recommendations and rationale**

Clients receive our recommendations and the reasoning behind them. No black boxes.

**Timeline and deliverables**

Clear expectations about what we will deliver and when.

**Scope boundaries**

Clients know what is included and what is not. We are explicit about limitations.

**How we work**

Clients understand our general approach, values, and communication style. We are transparent about our process without revealing operational details.

---

## Why This Separation Matters

**Protects the business**

Sharing internal financial models, qualification criteria, or strategic plans weakens our competitive position and exposes us to unnecessary risk.

**Maintains professionalism**

Clients hire us for our judgment and expertise. They do not need to see every internal deliberation or operational detail.

**Builds trust through relevant transparency**

Clients care about their project, their data, and the decisions they need to make. We are radically transparent about those things. They do not care about our internal systems.

**Allows honest internal conversation**

If every conversation is client-facing, we cannot have candid internal discussions about what is working and what is not.

**Prevents scope creep**

Sharing too much operational detail invites clients to weigh in on how we work, rather than focusing on the outcomes they need.

---

## Rule of Thumb for Deciding What Belongs Where

Ask this question:

**Does this information help the client make a better decision about their business?**

- **Yes**: Consider sharing it externally
- **No**: Keep it internal

Examples:

- "We found three data quality issues in your sales pipeline" (share)
- "Our internal project tracker shows this is taking longer than usual" (keep internal)
- "Here is how we structured the analysis and why" (share)
- "Here is our exact pricing model and margin calculation" (keep internal)
- "We recommend stopping this work because the data does not support it" (share)
- "This client scored low on our internal qualification rubric" (keep internal)

## Common Scenarios

**A client asks how we price our work**

Share: The published pricing for their tier (Starter: $1,500/month, Growth: $3,500/month, Enterprise: custom) and what it includes.  
Keep internal: The margin, cost breakdown, profitability analysis, or internal pricing strategy discussions.

**A client asks about upgrading from Starter to Growth**

Share: What additional capabilities they get, the price difference, and whether it fits their needs.  
Keep internal: Our internal assessment of whether they are a good fit for Growth, capacity constraints, or strategic account prioritization.

**A client asks why we declined a request**

Share: The scope boundary and why the request falls outside the engagement.  
Keep internal: The internal discussion or red flags that informed the decision.

**A client wants to understand our process**

Share: How we approach their specific problem and what they can expect.  
Keep internal: The detailed operational steps, tools, or internal checkpoints we use.

**A client asks if we have worked on similar problems**

Share: General experience and relevant case examples (anonymized).  
Keep internal: Specific client names, project details, or financials from past work.

**A client is frustrated with progress**

Share: What is taking time, what we are learning, and adjusted expectations.  
Keep internal: Internal team concerns, capacity constraints, or process breakdowns.

## Maintaining the Boundary

**Be deliberate**

Before sharing information, ask whether it serves the client or just feels transparent.

**Do not overshare**

Transparency is not the same as total openness. Share what matters, protect what does not.

**Avoid internal language in client settings**

Do not reference internal processes, scoring systems, or operational terms in client conversations.

**Separate internal and client-facing documents**

If a document is for internal use, label it clearly and do not send it to clients.

**Train the team on the boundary**

As the firm grows, everyone needs to understand what stays inside and what goes outside.

---

## When to Reconsider the Boundary

This framework will evolve. Reconsider what belongs where if:

- A client relationship requires more operational transparency to succeed
- Market expectations shift in a way that makes certain internal practices worth sharing
- We build a product or service that benefits from open-sourcing parts of our process
- Competitive dynamics change and transparency becomes a differentiator

For now, the rule is simple: share what helps the client decide, protect what helps us operate.

---

## Summary

**Internal**: How we run, decide, and improve.  
**External**: What the client needs to make better decisions.

This boundary protects both sides and keeps the focus where it belongs.
